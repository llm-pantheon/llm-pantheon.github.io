In response to some concerns on LessWrong that the results were purely due to noise, I ran an extra experiment. You can read the comment here: https://t.co/KYWJz63w10, but my summarized arguments for why it isn't noise:

1) We don't cherry-pick the layer range or steering strength for noise, they were based on some theories that predate the experiments.

2) Can steering inject noise? Yes, but this is very scale-dependent. For example, cranking steering up to 500x strength got this model to answer "Yes" 50% of the time for the bread injection even without info, but also corrupted its answers to the control questions. But for our experiments this confusion on the control questions doesn't happen at 20x vector scale.

(Why "this model" and "for our experiments"? Because steering strengths are sensitive to the vector and exact model, down to differences between base and post-train - e.g. base models often need half to a tenth of the steering strength of a post-trained model for similar steering effects. Or from Open Character Training: "For LLAMA 3.1 8B, QWEN 2.5 7B, and GEMMA 3 4B, we use vastly different steering constants of 0.7, 4.0, and 525.0, respectively, to produce similar responses." )

3) Similarly, partially to combat noise and partially to more clearly demonstrate that the model can introspect *past* states, we don't actively steer the model while it's answering, only during the first turn of KV cache generation. This means the steering vector can't directly act on the Yes / No logits we're measuring, the model needs to "pull it out" of the KV cache, reducing noise.

4) I think the inaccurate location results are particularly hard to explain via noise. The inaccurate location prompt (which falsely tells the model that the injection was only done to the assistant message, not to the whole first turn) starts with a *higher* baseline probability of Yes without steering, 3% vs 0.8% for the info prompt. But it only has less than half the Yes-shift under steering: 22% vs 53%. How do you explain that with noise, if steering just differentially noises e.g. questions the model is less sure about? Similarly with the Lorem Ipsum prompt - why would that have such a tiny shift despite being the same length as the info prompt, if all we care about is having more tokens to inject noise in?

4.5) Similarly, if it's *steering* noise, why would swapping in the EM finetune have such a similar introspective Yes-shift to steering with the EM vector in experiment 7? I would expect using a finetune would produce much less noise than unprincipled steering.

5) Finally, I ran a set of layer sweeps (inspired by experiments the commenter had previously run), showing that the patterning of Yes-shift due to injection was very different between the prompts that seemed to show introspection and the control question.

The control question's Yes probability is maximized by an injection starting about halfway through, and extending to the end, which makes sense if your strategy is to be pulled from KV into the active residual in ~the same layer and then influence lm_head. Meanwhile, the introspection injections are maximized by injecting *early* - which makes sense if your goal is to reach multiple introspection circuits throughout the model. (The "perfect" injection would be to just inject on layer 0 right after token embeddings - as if you'd given the model different input text. That'd be very easy to detect!)

The paper by @uzaymacar on introspective awareness mechanisms shows that base and SFT-only models do not have introspective ability with detecting feature injection. After DPO, the model gains the ability to introspect. Adding instruct RL doesn't help on top of DPO. They first checked OLMo (which has checkpoints for these stages already) and then ran DPO on Gemma base, finding that it leads to introspection. Though real Gemma has way more introspection than they got with DPO, so there's other techniques or data that can do a lot better than just this DPO setup.

What about DPO causes this? It might be the contrastive structure; training on pairs of (chosen, rejected). Perhaps it is the reference model, shrinking updates which deviate too much. Or maybe it's something specific to the DPO loss formula.

- Removing the reference model does nothing (i.e. retains introspection)
- Swapping DPO loss for other contrastive losses does nothing
- SFT on just the chosen responses in the would-be DPO pairs leads to no introspection, incl. w/ KL penalty
- They did not check if RL without DPO on base or SFT'd model is sufficient. This would be interesting to see!

What about DPO is causing introspection? The contrastive pairs contain stuff like two model responses rated for:
- helpfulness or honesty
- refusing "harmful" requests
- programming or reasoning solutions
- instruction-following
- etc.

If you randomly chose the response, you don't get introspection gains (noise). All categories of questions produce introspection. You can get introspection on e.g. code alone. The least helpful category is "safety." In fact, simply ablating the refusal direction massively improves introspection ability.

pushing a MoE model to 256K context on a single machine without losing speed. the trick is hybrid sliding window attention.

step-3.5-flash-REAP-121B: 121B total parameters, 11B active per token. Q6_K quantisation. running on llama-server with these flags:

~~~
llama-server \
  -m step-3.5-flash-121B-Q6_K.gguf \
  -ngl 99 -c 262144 -fa on \
  --cache-type-k q8_0 \
  --cache-type-v q8_0
~~~

the model uses hybrid SWA (sliding window attention) internally. most attention layers only look at a local window. a few “anchor” layers see the full context. 

result: 256K context at 12+ tok/s streaming with almost zero speed degradation as context fills up.

standard full attention at 256K would tank throughput. SWA keeps it constant by only computing full attention on the layers that need it.

this architecture pattern isn’t unique to step-3.5. qwen3.6 and gemma 4 MoE variants use similar hybrid attention. 

if you’re running any MoE locally and your speed drops as context grows, check whether your model supports SWA and whether your runtime is using it.

-fa on (flash attention). without it, none of this works.

speculative decoding is usually something you download. I wanted to know what it costs to train your own: an EAGLE-3 drafter for Gemma-3-27B, trained from scratch on one RTX 5090, in four nights.

measured speedup over plain decoding, at the best checkpoint:

- code: 1.44x (59.6 to 89.0 tok/s by the final bench)
- repetitive text: 1.52x
- prose: 1.33x
- chat: 1.23x

~~~
the drafter guesses a few tokens ahead and the 27B verifies them in one pass. verified tokens are exactly what the big model would have produced, so output quality does not move. the speedup is decided by how many guesses survive verification: accept length here reached 1.97 tokens per pass on repetitive text, 1.56 on prose.

~~~
also, interesting: more training made it worse. the curve climbed for three nights (repetitive 1.37x to 1.44x to 1.52x), then night four regressed prose from 1.33x to 1.24x. peak was 21000 steps, 39% of one epoch.
